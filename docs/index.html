Keep what matters smooth, without closing anything.

Coremium lives in your Mac's notch. While you play, render, code or run a local model, it quietly moves everything else to the efficiency cores. You choose which apps get out of the way.

Download for Mac Read the source

Free, open source, no account, no network access. macOS 13 or later, Apple Silicon and Intel.

Your Mac has two kinds of cores. Apps rarely share them well.

Performance cores are fast and power-hungry. Efficiency cores are calm and frugal. When a game and a dozen background apps all pile onto the fast cores, the game waits its turn.

Coremium points your background apps at the efficient ones while the app in front keeps the fast ones. When you're done, everything goes back to how macOS had it.

Four choices for every app.

Coremium picks sensible defaults from what each app is. Change any of them whenever you like. Your choice always beats the mode.

Boost
Protected. When you use it, everything else is kept out of its way. Your game, your editor, your render.
Normal
Left alone. Coremium never touches it.
Yield
Steps aside to the efficiency cores while something else is boosted. Chrome, chat, a sync app.
Eco
Always on the efficiency cores, unless you're using it. Backups and things you never need fast.

It tells you why.

Automatic mode watches what you're doing and says what it changed, so it never feels like a black box. Turn on Advanced for the numbers: per-core load, memory, process counts and which rule applies to each app.

Pause puts everything back to macOS defaults straight away.

Gaming mode activatedRoblox became active. Chrome and Discord moved to Yield, Spotify to Eco.

Back to normalNothing to protect right now. Every app runs at default priority.

PausedmacOS default scheduling restored. Nothing is being moved.

What it doesn't do.

It never quits or freezes an app. It only touches your own user's apps, with no root helper and no network access.

macOS has no way to push an app above normal priority, so Boost works by clearing the way for it. If your Mac isn't overloaded, there is little to move.

It doesn't promise more frames, longer battery life or a cooler Mac, because it can't measure those. It shows what it can measure: how long a boost ran and how many processes it moved.

Quitting Coremium, or running its restore command, puts everything back.

Install it in a minute.

Download the disk image

  1. Open the downloaded Coremium disk image and drag Coremium onto Applications.
  2. Open it. Coremium is signed without a paid Apple developer account, so macOS asks once: open System Settings, Privacy & Security, and choose Open Anyway.
  3. Follow the short tour, then hover the notch.

Checksums are published with every release.

Prefer Terminal? This downloads the latest release, checks its checksum and installs it, with no warning from macOS.

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Cubinghackerz/Coremium/master/scripts/install.sh | bash

Something stays slow? /Applications/Coremium.app/Contents/MacOS/Coremium --restore-all puts every app back.
